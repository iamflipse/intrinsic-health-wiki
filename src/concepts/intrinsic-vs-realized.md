# Intrinsic vs Realized Health

Health is not only the state of the body — it is also the **context** that body lives in: values, preferences, environment, culture, perceptions, behaviors, lifestyle. Cohen calls this the **"lens of context."**

## The car-accident thought experiment

Two 45-year-old women, both generally healthy, both made paraplegic by the same accident:

| | Writer | Marathon runner |
|---|---|---|
| Lifestyle fit | Not sporty; lives in an accessible, wheelchair-friendly city; wealthy enough to adapt | Identity built on marathon running; mountainous town with few accessible buildings; not wealthy |
| Impact on health | Small | Large |

Even with **no meaningful difference in physiological state**, most people agree the accident hit the second woman's health far harder. That gap is **realized health**.

## The distinction

- **Intrinsic health:** the state of the body *as it permits health*, generally speaking (the body's capacity to maintain dynamic equilibrium — see [first principles](first-principles-organisms.md)).
- **Realized health:** health **as we experience it**, through our individual lens of context.

Realized health is "a whole academic discipline in itself"; this post focuses on the intrinsic side.

**Discussion question (Forlenza):** is realized health a function of intrinsic health *constrained by* social, psychological, economic, and environmental affordances? See [Discussion & Critiques](discussion-critique.md).
